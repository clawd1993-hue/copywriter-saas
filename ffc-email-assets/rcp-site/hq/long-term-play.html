0 · One-paragraph summary

Coaching/install business = the wedge. It earns cash now (pilot → $5k/mo continuity), proves the "RIA Operating System", and shows which RIAs are actually good. Roll-up = the payday: graduate the top ~15–20 OS-compliant RIAs into an option-based pool (8x EBITDA floor, shared uplift above it, drag-along), then sell the pool + the platform to PE. Aggressive-but-plausible year-7 outcome: ~$77M gross (platform $32M + roll-up carry $45M), ≈25% of ~$302M total value created. 5–7 year game. "$5M I keep" is roughly year 2–3 of the platform alone, so the near goal and this map don't conflict.

1 · The exit path (Hormozi, 2026-11-12, verbatim)

Short answer: the cleanest exit with a "Gym Launch for RIAs" is not selling the coaching business by itself. It's using the coaching business to create a standardized pool of RIAs you have options/equity in, then selling that pool plus your platform to PE at a higher multiple.

Think: coaching business = wedge. Roll-up = payday.

Step 0: Define the model (Gym Launch → RIA version) Gym Launch structure in RIA clothes: - Front end: free trainings, books, audits for RIAs on growth / capacity / succession. Each free asset points to a "let us help you implement" call. ($100M Money Models, p.148) - Core offer: high-ticket, 16-week implementation intensive for RIAs (done-with-you growth & ops install). Think "$X over 16 weeks" with you as the premium price leader. ($100M Offers, p.55) - Back end: annual continuity for advanced firms (playbooks, community, quarterly planning, hiring help, tech stack, compliance-friendly marketing library). This is your "Gym Lords" equivalent. ($100M Money Models, p.149) This gives you cash now (intensive) + recurring revenue (continuity) + deep visibility into which RIAs are actually good.

Phase 1: First 10 RIAs – proof & playbook Goal: prove the offer and build the "RIA Operating System." - Sell the first RIA a 16-week intensive at a real price (not cheap "beta"). Deliver obvious AUM / revenue / profit lift. - Document everything: scripts, emails, workflows, hiring profiles, tech stack, meeting cadence. - Get 3–5 hard case studies: "Firm grew EBITDA by $Y, margins to Z%, owner hours cut by A." You're not thinking roll-up yet. You're earning the right by becoming the best in this one game. I did 30+ gym turnarounds before I licensed anything.

Phase 2: 10–50 RIAs – productize & remove you Goal: turn you from hero to system. - Move delivery from 1:1 consulting to a program: semi-private cohorts, standardized curriculum, templates, calculators, checklists. - Install simple funnel: Opt-in → VSL → Application → Closer. No random calls, no custom projects. - Hire a coach/operator to run the OS for clients. Your job: refine the machine, not fulfil every call. Now you have a real business, not a freelancer gig. This is what buyers eventually pay for: independence and predictability.

Phase 3: 50–200 RIAs – turn platform into a future roll-up Goal: build a pool of "roll-up-ready" RIAs running your OS. - Narrow avatar: e.g. RIAs with $100M–$1B AUM, fee-based, recurring revenue, similar tech stack. - Enforce the same operating system across all clients: CRM, reporting, org chart, meeting cadence, P&L format. - Track them all centrally. You now know whose EBITDA is strong, whose clients are sticky, and who wants out in 3–5 years. At this point you can just sell the platform (like we did with Gym Launch), or you run the roll-up play for a bigger check.

Phase 4: Introduce the "option-based roll-up" Only for your top ~20 RIAs: - Offer: "We'll waive most fees, help you acquire smaller books, and prep you for exit. In exchange, we get an option on X% of your firm if/when we sell the group." - Requirements: they run your OS fully (so they're plug-and-play to a buyer); they reduce founder dependence (you don't want 'key man' businesses). - Add a simple drag-along clause across participants: if an offer exceeds a set multiple, everyone agrees to sell together. You're quietly assembling one niche, one operating system, many RIAs. That's what PE wants.

Phase 5: Package and exit When the pool hits meaningful scale (e.g. combined EBITDA / AUM that puts you in "platform" territory): - Get clean financials and cohort data across the group. - Hire an investment banker who already does RIA deals. - Take them a story: "X RIAs, Y AUM, all on one OS, with options that let a buyer consolidate." - Sell: your coaching/platform company (recurring revenue); your equity/options in the underlying RIAs; roll some equity into the newco if you want a second bite.

Timeline reality: this is a 5–7 year game from first client if you execute like a psychopath and don't chase shiny objects. If you want, next step I can map rough revenue targets and headcount by phase so you know what "on track" looks like year by year.

2 · The 7-year math (Hormozi, 2026-11-12, verbatim)

Let's run one aggressive-but-plausible 7-year scenario and do the math. Assumptions first, then your walk-away.

1. What "doing really well" looks like by year 7

A. The "Gym Launch for RIAs" platform business - 250+ RIA clients have been through your system. - 120 are on continuity at an average of $4k/month. - You run events, licensing, etc. and sit at $8M revenue / $4M EBITDA with a lean group delivery model. ($100M Playbook Lifetime Value, p.6, p.8) - A strategic buyer or PE-backed aggregator pays 8x EBITDA for a high-margin, low-capital, recurring-revenue education / platform biz. - Value of platform: $32M.

B. The RIA roll-up pool From your client base, you've graduated a subset into an option-based pool: - 15 RIAs in one tight niche (say $500M–$2B AUM each, same OS). - Average EBITDA per RIA: $1.5M. Combined EBITDA pool: $22.5M. - On their own, these firms could probably sell around 8x EBITDA to a normal buyer (the standalone market rate).

You structure your deals exactly like the playbook pattern. Each owner signs an option: - Floor: they are guaranteed 8x EBITDA on sale. - Upside: any multiple above 8x gets split; you keep a set slice of the "extra."

You go to PE with: one niche, one OS, $22.5M EBITDA, pre-wired options. PE loves simplicity and scale in RIAs. A clean, at-scale, systematized platform can realistically clear 12x EBITDA in this environment if it's actually high quality. - Roll-up sale price: 12x × $22.5M = $270M.

The "arbitrage" you created: - Base value at 8x: $180M. Actual sale at 12x: $270M. Uplift = $90M that exists only because you standardized and packaged them. - You negotiate that you keep 2x EBITDA of that uplift (half) via your options: your carry = 2 × $22.5M = $45M. - Advisors effectively walk with the other 10x (their 8x floor + 2x uplift), a great deal for them too.

Two checks: $32M from selling the platform company + $45M from the roll-up arbitrage. Total gross before taxes: $77M.

2. What % of the total pie is that? Roll-up $270M + platform $32M = $302M in play. Your cut $77M ≈ 25%+ of the total value created in the ecosystem, with zero need to run any RIA day to day.

3. Reality check Is this guaranteed? No. What has to be true: - You actually get 15 legit RIAs to full OS compliance. - You protect EBITDA (no bloated coaching org). - You negotiate the option and uplift economics cleanly from day one. - You stay obsessed with LTV and margin so you can self-fund growth and get to those 250+ RIA relationships. ($100M Playbook Lifetime Value p.6, Retention p.13)

But directionally: a 7-year "psychopath focus" run could put you in a $50M–$100M personal exit outcome if you hit something like the numbers above.
