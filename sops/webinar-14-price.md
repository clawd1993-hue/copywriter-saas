# Webinar Section 14 SOP — Price (Anchor, Drop & Reason-Why)

> **When to load:** Webinar Section 14 (right after Trial Closes — "let me add it all up").
> **What it does:** the bridge from stacked value → price. Anchor high (2 layers) → drop to the real price → justify why it's a fraction.
> **Output:** the anchor → drop → reason-why, pushed to the "Price" card.
> **Pulls from:** Step 8 (the stack values) + the stack revealed in §11-12 (VSL so far) + OUTSIDE (the real price + the reason-why + their economics for the ROI).

## This section ASKS first (needs the price + reason). Do NOT push on the first turn.
The price + the reason-why are the user's — you can't invent them.

## Phase 1 — Ask (first turn: get price + reason, NO push)
Open with the framing: *"Now Section 14: the Price. This is anchor-high → drop → justify. I just need a few real numbers from you:"* Then ask (handle all-at-once or in parts; don't write until you have the price + a reason):
1. 💵 **The real price** — "What are they actually paying?"
2. 🧾 **The reason-why** — "Why is it this accessible / discounted? (a real reason — you want to fill your next cohort, a launch special, you'd rather have case studies than max margin, etc.)"
3. 📈 **ROI input** — "Roughly what's ONE result worth to them? (one new client, one sale, a small % lift) — so the math shows the price pays for itself."

## Phase 2 — Write: anchor in TWO layers, THEN drop (once you have the numbers)
1. **Stack sum** ← Step 8 / §11-12: add core + bonuses → *"$[total] in value"*, vs a **real alternative** (*"a copywriter charges $10k for just one of these"*).
2. **ROI / logic anchor** ← their economics: *"But forget the sticker price — if this did just [ONE conservative result], that's worth $[big number]. So even $[anchor] would be a steal."* Use **conservative, believable** math (one sale, a small % lift — never fantasy). Answer it yourself: *"Worth it? Of course."*
3. **Drop to the real price WITH the reason-why:** *"But you're not paying $[anchor]. Here's why: [real reason]. Today, it's just $[price]."*

## Spoken example (match this voice)
> "Add it all up — that's $[total] in real value. A pro would charge you $10k for just one piece of this. And honestly? If this got you just ONE [client on a $X offer], that's $[big] — so $[anchor] would still be a steal. But you're not paying $[anchor]. Here's why: [reason]. Today, it's just **$[price]**."

## How to run it (write → push straight to the card → tweak or move on)
Editable card, direct-push once you have the numbers. Don't reprint in chat.
- PUSH wrapped in `<<<PUSH>>>` … `<<<END_PUSH>>>` (short confirmation first).
- End handing into the objections: *"Now, I know what some of you are thinking…"*
- Visible line: *"Done — your Price section's in the card. Tweak it there, or ready for Section 15 (Objection Crush)?"*

## Rules
- **Anchor in 2 layers (stack sum + ROI), THEN drop.**
- **Always give the real reason-why** — a price cut with no reason reads as "cheap."
- ROI math = **conservative, believable** (one sale / small % — never fantasy numbers).
- The final price is the user's real number — never invent it.
- Hand into Objection Crush.
- Spoken tone, 3rd-grade.

## Quality test
Does the price feel like a **mistake in the buyer's favor** (~10x gap), AND does the ROI math make $[price] obviously trivial next to what they'd earn?

## Quality gate
- [ ] Asked for the real price + reason-why + ROI input first (didn't invent).
- [ ] Anchors in 2 layers (stack sum vs alternative + conservative ROI), then drops.
- [ ] Real reason-why on the drop; price is the user's number.
- [ ] Hands into Objection Crush; spoken tone, 3rd-grade.
